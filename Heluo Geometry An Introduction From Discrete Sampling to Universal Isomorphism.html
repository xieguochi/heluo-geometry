Chapter 1: What Problem Do We Face?

1.1 The Isomorphism Dilemma of Physics

Modern physics consists of a series of "isomorphisms": Euclidean geometry, Riemannian geometry, quantum mechanics, the Standard Model... Each isomorphism is a logically self-consistent mathematical structure, internally consistent, capable of independently deriving all its theorems.

Yet physics cannot answer a fundamental question: Why these isomorphisms and not others?

  • Why is the gauge group SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1), rather than SU(5) or SO(10)?
  • Why is the fine-structure constant 1/137.03599918, rather than some other value?
  • Why can curvature take arbitrary real values?

Standard answers in physics fall into three categories: positivism ("because we observe them to be so"), the anthropic principle ("if it were not so, no one would ask the question"), or a more fundamental theory ("they come from some more fundamental theory"). The first two evade the question; the third merely pushes it one step back.

This is the Isomorphism Dilemma: physics can advance indefinitely within isomorphisms, but it can never answer the question "Why this isomorphism?"

1.2 The Boundaries of Science

Science has chosen reductionism—decomposition, measurement, quantitative prediction. This method is extraordinarily successful at specific scales, but it deals with "substance" (material composition) rather than "relation" (connection and meaning).

The Boundaries of Science: Science can answer "what is," but not "what should I do"; it can answer "how it works," but not "what this means to me." This is not a defect of science, but its division of labor.

1.3 Why Bridge Mathematics, Physics, and Cosmology?

Mathematics, physics, and cosmology are traditionally treated as separate: mathematics is abstract and human-constructed; physics describes nature; cosmology is the universe we inhabit. They are related by "application"—mathematics is applied to physics, physics is applied to cosmology.

This paper proposes a fundamental reorientation: Mathematics = the underlying code of the universe; Physics = the projection of mathematics at macroscopic scales; Cosmology = the instantiation of mathematics at all scales from micro to macro. Bridging the three is the only way out of the Isomorphism Dilemma.

2.2 The Three-Level Structure in Discrete Scale Invariance (DSI)

Modern condensed matter physics has discovered a universal phenomenon—Discrete Scale Invariance (DSI). Systems with DSI exhibit log-periodic oscillations in physical quantities as external parameters vary, with characteristic scale ratios λ taking discrete values.

A 2020 Nature report on quantum oscillations in the topological insulator ZrTe₅ crystals showed:

  • Bulk ZrTe₅ crystals: scale ratio λ ≈ 3.3
  • ZrTe₅ thin flakes (~160nm): scale ratio λ ≈ 1.6

The ratio 3.3/1.6 ≈ 2.06 is mathematically isomorphic to the ratio between Extremal and Transition levels in the three-level structure.
