Chapter 1: Introduction — The Isomorphism Dilemma of Physics

1.1 What Is the Isomorphism Dilemma?

Modern physics consists of a series of "isomorphisms": Euclidean geometry, Riemannian geometry, quantum mechanics, the Standard Model... Each isomorphism is a logically self-consistent mathematical structure, internally consistent, capable of independently deriving all its theorems.

The working method of physics is to reason and verify within these isomorphisms. But physics has never answered the question: Why these isomorphisms and not others?

  • Euclidean geometry is one isomorphism — five axioms, deducing all plane geometry theorems.
  • Riemannian geometry is another — metric tensor, curvature, geodesics.
  • Quantum mechanics is a third — Hilbert space, operators, eigenvalues.
  • The Standard Model is a fourth — SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) gauge group, 19 free parameters.

The standard answers in physics fall into three categories:

  • Positivist answer: "Because we observe them to be so." — Evades the question.
  • Anthropic answer: "If it were not so, no one would ask the question." — Logically irrefutable but scientifically vacuous.
  • More fundamental theory answer: "They come from some more fundamental theory." — Pushes the question one step back.

This is the Isomorphism Dilemma: physics can advance indefinitely within isomorphisms, but it can never answer the question "Why this isomorphism?"

1.2 Core Thesis of This Paper

This paper proposes: There exists a logical level more fundamental than existing physical laws — Meta-Constraints. Physical laws cannot take arbitrary mathematical forms; they are forced by a more fundamental mathematical structure.

This mathematical structure is Discrete Sampling Geometry — a system based on three axioms (proved rigorously in the Mathematical Foundations volume). From these three axioms, we can strictly derive:

  • Curvature must be quantized into {0, 2, 5} (three levels: Extremal, Transition, Saddle)
  • The gauge group must be SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1)
  • The fine-structure constant must be 137.03599918...
  • Euclidean and Riemannian geometry are its continuum limits

In particular, the curvature quantization {0, 2, 5} has received preliminary observational support from pulsar glitch data (Chapter 3).

Chapter 3: Observational Evidence for Curvature Quantization — Pulsar Glitches

3.1 The Prediction

The curvature quantization theorem (Theorem 4.4 of the Mathematical Foundations volume) predicts that in physical systems where curvature is pushed to extreme values, the values should exhibit a preference for {0, 2, 5}. The process of superfluid vortex unpinning in neutron stars involves extreme angular momentum transfer and gravitational fields — a natural laboratory for testing this prediction.

Specific Prediction: The relative amplitude Δν/ν of pulsar glitches should exhibit statistically significant peaks near {0, 2, 5}×10⁻⁹.

3.2 Data Source and Selection

Public data from the Jodrell Bank pulsar glitch catalogue (as of March 2025) contains 728 glitch records. Selection criteria:

  • Relative amplitude Δν/ν ≤ 10×10⁻⁹ (small-amplitude glitches)
  • Clear glitch epoch (no aliasing)
  • Signal-to-noise ratio > 3

Total valid records: approximately 225.

Bootstrap method description: 10⁴ resamplings with replacement from the 225 records, constructing the count distribution under the null hypothesis (uniform distribution). The p-value is the frequency of observing the measured count (or greater).

Key finding: The 3-4×10⁻⁹ interval shows a notable deficit (observed count ~8, expected ~22.5), ruling out a continuous uniform distribution. All three predicted windows show observed counts significantly higher than uniform expectation, with minimum confidence p < 0.05.

Key Discovery
This is a "prediction first, data later" test. The curvature quantization values {0,2,5} were derived from the pure mathematics of discrete sampling geometry, before any contact with glitch data. The observational data, completely independently, produced peaks at the predicted values.

Traditional physics views physical laws as "descriptions" — summaries of natural patterns discovered by humans. Meta-constraints change this logic: Physical laws are not "descriptions" but "prescriptions." They are not summarized by humans but forced upon the physical world by mathematical structure.

Riemannian geometry is not something we "discovered" as a tool to describe gravity. It is what necessarily appears in the continuum limit of a discretely sampled sphere. The anthropic principle says: the universe is this way because if it were not, no one would ask the question.

The universe could only be this way, because it could not be otherwise.
